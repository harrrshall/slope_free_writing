I'll have this one fixed when I do. I reached my hand. Better leave it now. I'll bring it back later. He gave me the watch. I put it in my pocket. I couldn't hear it now, above all the others. I'm much obliged to you. I hope I haven't taken up your time. That's all right. Bring it in when you are ready. And you better put off this celebration until after we win that boat race. Yes, sir. I reckon I had. I went out, shutting the door upon the ticking. I looked back into the window. He was watching me across the barrier. There were about a dozen watches in the window, a dozen different hours and each with the same assertive and contradictory assurance that mine had, without any hands at all. Contradicting one another. I could hear mine, ticking away inside my pocket, even though nobody could see it, even though it could tell nothing if anyone could. And so I told myself to take that one. Because Father said clocks slay time. He said time is dead as long as it is being clicked off by little wheels; only when the clock stops does time come to life. The hands were extended, slightly off the horizontal at a faint angle, like a gull tilting into the wind.
