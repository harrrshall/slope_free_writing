Isolde took center stage in a dress that sparkled like the morning dew. A long banner had been hung around the city in preparation of the tournament. The day was filled with music and games, jousting and food. Sir Edmond's greatest joust was against a black knight and though he was slain and sent to the ground Sir Gervais had placed his hand on the horse's eye to spare him further injury and humiliation. There were many stories to be told, but not as many as those told by Lady Isolde. All day long the sound of horses' hooves and clashing swords filled the air. Knights would charge at one another at full tilt, trying to topple each other from their saddles. The crowd cheered with gusto, their bloodlust satisfied by the sight of a fallen knight. The winner was often cheered by the crowd. Elara, watching the knights from her window, couldn't help but be drawn to Sir Gervais, the man her father had grown up alongside. But Lady Isolde was watching her daughter's interest in the knight, and she would not allow any potential for romance to form in the house that was hers. Elara, you have been drawn to Sir Gervais and the only knight to watch over you has been your father, her mother said. The day was spent with Lady Isolde being rude and brash and Elara having to be the voice of reason. When night fell and the feast began, a great fire had been set up and the sounds of merriment could be heard all over the kingdom. Sir Edmond was a knight who fought his way to glory and though he was not the only knight at the feast, his performance at the tournament was impressive. However, Lady Isolde had something else to say about it, a more theatrical version, a more dramatic version of what had transpired.
