Rain lashed against the windows of the old Victorian house, each drop a tiny hammer blow against the dusty glass. Inside, Eleanor huddled deeper into her armchair, a worn copy of Edgar Allan Poe clutched in her hands, the flickering candlelight casting long, dancing shadows across the room. Thunder grumbled in the distance, a low, guttural growl that echoed the unease churning in her stomach. She wasn't usually afraid of storms, but this one felt different, charged with a palpable tension that prickled the hairs on the back of her neck. The house, perched precariously on the windswept cliffs overlooking the Atlantic, was a monument to bygone grandeur, now swallowed by encroaching neglect. It had been her grandmother's, a woman of eccentricities and whispered secrets, who had passed away a month ago, leaving Eleanor its sole inheritor. Eleanor, a writer struggling to finish her debut novel, had seen it as a refuge, a sanctuary to escape the city's relentless hum and find inspiration in the solitude. Now, alone in its cavernous emptiness, it felt more like a tomb. The wind howled, rattling the ancient panes, and a floorboard groaned above her, the sound amplified in the stillness. Eleanor glanced up, her heart giving a nervous jolt. It had been like this since she arrived, a constant symphony of creaks and whispers, as if the house itself were sighing with age and sorrow. She dismissed it as settling sounds, the natural groans of a building weathered by centuries, but a shiver snaked down her spine nonetheless. The storm raged on, the rain turning the world outside into a swirling grey canvas. Darkness fell, swallowing the last sliver of daylight, and the candlelight flickered precariously, casting elongated, grotesque shadows that writhed like phantoms against the walls.
