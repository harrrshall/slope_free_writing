In the roaring twenties, when jazz filled the air and flappers danced the night away, there was a small, seedy theater in the heart of New York City's theater district that was home to a very different kind of entertainment. The Laughing Leopard Lounge was a place where aspiring comedians came to hone their craft, and where the city's most jaded audiences came to forget their troubles for a little while. The owner of this humble establishment was a man named Max, a former vaudeville performer with a thick mustache and a thicker accent, who had seen the rise and fall of countless comics over the years. He had a keen eye for talent, but an even keener ear for laughter. Every night, he sat in the back row, a cigar clenched between his teeth, listening for that special something that would make a comedian stand out from the rest. One rainy evening, a scrawny, nervous young man named Leo stepped onto the stage.
