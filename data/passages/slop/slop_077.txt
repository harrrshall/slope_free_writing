The year was 1927, but the world felt more akin to a rusted cog in a forgotten machine than the roaring twenties everyone whispered about in hushed, forbidden tones. The Great Collapse, they called it, a cataclysm that had swallowed the world whole, leaving behind a landscape of ash-choked cities and skeletal skyscrapers piercing the perpetual smog. The air tasted metallic, a constant reminder of the factories that churned day and night, spewing out the synthetic necessities that sustained the dwindling population. Eleanor clutched her ration card tighter, its worn edges familiar under her calloused fingers. It was her lifeline, granting her a meager allowance of nutrient paste and recycled fabric each week. The queue snaked through the concrete canyons, a writhing mass of faces etched with exhaustion and resignation. Above, the omnipresent screens of the Central Authority flickered, displaying propaganda that lauded the efficiency of the Collective and the virtues of conformity. Eleanor longed for the stories her grandmother used to tell, tales of a time before the Collapse, when the sky was blue, laughter came freely, and people weren't mere cogs in a machine. But those were relics of a forgotten past, as distant and unreal as a faded photograph. She finally reached the distribution point, a hulking metal contraption that dispensed rations with robotic precision. A gaunt man with eyes like hollowed-out sockets scanned her card and shoved a block of paste and a threadbare shawl into her bag. Remember, citizen, he rasped, his voice as dry as the dust that perpetually coated everything, deviation is not tolerated. Stay within the designated zones, report any anomalies, and contribute your quota to the Collective. Your duty is to the state. Eleanor nodded numbly, her gaze drifting towards the forbidden zone beyond the city walls. It was a desolate wasteland, shrouded in perpetual twilight, where rumors whispered of hidden pockets of resistance, of whispers of freedom.
