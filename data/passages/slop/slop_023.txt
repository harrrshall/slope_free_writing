The cobblestones of Florence hummed with the chaotic symphony of a city awake. Sunlight, filtered through a haze of chimney smoke and the pungent aroma of roasting chestnuts, painted the midday street in hues of saffron and sepia. A gaggle of schoolchildren, their laughter echoing off the frescoed walls of palazzi, jostled past Bartolomeo as he bent over his workbench, a symphony of his own, a quiet hum of metal on metal. Bartolomeo, a coppersmith of modest repute, prided himself on his quiet craftsmanship, a counterpoint to the raucous life of the Florentine streets. Today, however, the familiar rhythm of his work was interrupted by a woman unlike any he'd encountered before. Tall and slender, she moved with an ethereal grace, her crimson silk gown a single crimson splash against the muted palette of the marketplace. Her skin, pale as alabaster, seemed to glow faintly under the Tuscan sun, and her silver hair, threaded with pearls the colour of moonlight, cascaded down her back in a silken waterfall. She had eyes the colour of twilight, fathomless pools that seemed to hold both starlight and shadows. She was a creature plucked from a tapestry, not a living woman belonging to the cobblestones and grime of Florence. She stopped before his stall, her presence stirring a prickle of unease in Bartolomeo's normally placid heart.
