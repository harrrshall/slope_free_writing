The flickering gaslight cast long, dancing shadows across the smoky interior of The Speakeasy, a hidden jazz den tucked away in the belly of Chicago. A haze of cigarette smoke and illicit laughter hung heavy in the air, mingling with the intoxicating scent of bootleg gin and the rhythmic pulse of a ragtime piano. At the center of it all stood Beatrice Bea Bellweather, a woman whose wit was as sharp as the flapper dress she wore, its fringed hem swaying with the infectious energy of the music. Bea was no ordinary flapper; she was the reigning queen of The Speakeasy's weekly comedy night, a haven for jokesters and dreamers seeking refuge from the grim realities of the Prohibition era. Tonight, however, Bea felt a tremor of anxiety gnawing at her usual confidence. A stranger sat at a table in the back, his face obscured by a wide-brimmed fedora, radiating an aura of quiet menace. Rumors whispered through the room about his reputation - a ruthless businessman, a known bootlegger, a man who laughed only when his enemies stumbled. This man, known only as The Shadow, had arrived just as Bea was preparing to take the stage, and his presence cast a long, unwelcome shadow over the usually jovial atmosphere.
