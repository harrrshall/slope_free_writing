The room was in disarray, furniture overturned, paintings ripped from the walls, leaving gaping holes in the plaster. Moonlight sliced through a broken window, illuminating dust motes dancing in the swirling air. The scraping sound came again, accompanied by a wet, sickening thump. Finch, gun drawn, cautiously entered, his boots crunching on shattered glass. In the center of the room, bathed in the pale moonlight, stood a figure hunched over something on the floor. As Finch stepped closer, the figure straightened, revealing a woman in a blood-soaked dress, her face pale and drawn, eyes wide with a feral gleam. In her arms, she cradled a mangled, unrecognizable shape, its limbs contorted at unnatural angles. The stench of rot and decay hit him like a physical blow. Who are you? Finch demanded, his voice hoarse. The woman didn't answer, but let out a guttural moan, a sound that scraped against his sanity like nails on a chalkboard. She clutched the shape tighter, her eyes never leaving his, a chilling hunger burning within them. Finch, despite his years on the force, felt a primal fear coil in his gut. This wasn't a human. It was something else, something twisted and monstrous. He realized with a sickening certainty that the whispers, the stories, weren't just folklore. Grimley Street held a truth far older, far darker, than he could have ever imagined. Suddenly, the woman lunged, the shape in her arms transforming into a writhing mass of claws and teeth. Finch reacted instinctively, firing his revolver. The shots echoed through the empty house, the bullets finding their mark with sickening thuds. The woman screamed, a high-pitched, inhuman wail that reverberated through his bones. She staggered back, clutching her wounds, the creature in her arms dissolving into a cloud of black smoke. Finch stood there, panting, the smell of gunpowder and decay heavy in the air. The woman, weakened but still alive, snarled at him, her eyes burning with a cold, ancient fury. But before she could recover, a wave of energy erupted from her, throwing Finch back against the wall. He felt a searing pain explode in his chest, his vision blurring. He struggled to stay conscious, his last thought a chilling certainty: Grimley Street would never be truly cleansed.
