In the shadowed streets of a city where the sun barely pierced the smog, a young woman named Eliza trudged through the rain, her feet sinking into the mud. The year was 1925, but it felt like a century ago, a time long forgotten. The city, once a beacon of progress, had fallen into decay, its grand buildings now crumbling under the weight of neglect and despair. Eliza's eyes, a piercing blue, scanned the gloomy surroundings. She was searching for something, or rather, someone. Her mission was dangerous, and she knew it. The authorities, known as the Overseers, had eyes everywhere. They monitored every move, every whisper, every thought. The slightest hint of dissent could mean imprisonment or worse. Her target was a man named Marcus, a former scientist who had been exiled for his radical theories. Eliza had heard whispers of his work, of a device that could change the world, a device that could bring light back into the darkened city. She needed to find him, to convince him to come back and share his knowledge. As she walked, the rain began to fall harder, the sound a constant drum against the cobblestones. Eliza's clothes stuck to her skin, and her breath formed small clouds of mist in the cold air. She reached a dilapidated building, its windows shattered, its walls cracked. This was the place, she knew. Marcus had been seen here before. With a deep breath, she pushed open the creaky door and stepped inside. The building was dark, the only light coming from a flickering oil lamp in the corner. The air was thick with the smell of decay and damp. Eliza's footsteps echoed through the empty hallways, each step a potential warning to the Overseers. She found a narrow staircase, its steps worn and uneven. With a sense of urgency, she began to climb, her hand gripping the rusted rail. At the top, she paused, listening for any sign of life. Then she heard it, a faint whisper of conversation. She crept closer, her heart pounding in her chest.
