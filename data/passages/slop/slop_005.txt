The first drops of rain began to splatter against the ancient cobblestones, each one a whispered secret to the night. The streetlights flickered, their glow as unreliable as the neon signs in the pawn shop window. The neighborhood had seen better days; now, it was a place where hope went to die. Molly stood at the window, her reflection staring back at her, framed by the dusty panes. Her once-vibrant red hair was now a dull rust, streaked with silver. Her eyes, once green, were faded to the color of faded jade, reflecting the gloom outside. She was dressed in her late mother's housedress, the print faded from years of wear. The clock ticked away the minutes, each one a slow drip of sand in an hourglass. The doorbell rang, a harsh jarring sound that echoed through the empty house. Molly jumped, her hand flying to her heart. Calm down, she muttered to herself, It's just the pizza. She didn't have visitors these days, not since her mother's passing. She'd become a hermit, holing herself up in the old house, surrounded by the ghosts of her past. She opened the door to find a young man on her doorstep, hood drawn up against the rain, rainwater dripping from the peak.
