In the heart of Manchester, where smoke from the factories blanketed the sky and the thrum of machinery never ceased, lived a young woman named Elizabeth. She was the daughter of a mill owner, a woman of privilege despite the harsh reality of the Industrial Revolution that surrounded her. Elizabeth's days were spent managing the household, tending to her younger siblings, and attending to the needs of her father's guests, who were often wealthy industrialists from across the country. Among these guests was a man named Edward, the son of a rival mill owner. He was a year or so older than Elizabeth, with dark, rebellious hair and eyes that sparkled with a mix of intelligence and mischief. Edward was a man who defied the norms of his time, preferring the company of books to the latest technological marvels, and the company of artists and thinkers to the elite gatherings his father expected him to attend.
