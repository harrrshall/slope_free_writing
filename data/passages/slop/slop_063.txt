The days turned into a week, then two. The girl stayed, nursing her drinks, watching the door, waiting. Eli tended to her, poured her drinks, but he kept his distance, his silence. The saloon patrons, drawn to her like flies to a flame, tried to engage her, to draw her into their games, their stories. But she remained steadfast, her gaze fixed on the entrance, her mind focused on her purpose. Then, one day, as the sun hung low in the sky, casting long shadows across the dusty street, the saloon doors swung open. In walked a man, his boots scuffed, his clothes dusty from the trail. His face, weathered by time and sun, held a familiar warmth, a smile that could light up the darkest night. He was a stranger, and yet, there was something achingly familiar about him. The saloon fell silent once more, all eyes turning to the man. He stepped inside, his gaze scanning the room, then landing on the girl in the corner. He froze, his smile fading, his eyes widening in recognition. Kat? he whispered, his voice barely audible, yet it echoed through the silent saloon.
