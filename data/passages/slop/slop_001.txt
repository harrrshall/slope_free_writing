The sun dipped low on the horizon, casting a golden glow over the cobbled streets of Florence. It was the year 1513, and the city was a hub of art and intellect, a place where the whispers of the old world mingled with the promise of a new age. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth and fresh paint, a testament to the endless construction and renovation that characterized this era of rebirth. In the heart of the city, the grand duomo rose majestically, its unfinished dome a symbol of aspirations yet to be realized. The bustling marketplace was alive with vendors calling out their wares, the vibrant colors of their goods contrasting against the earthy tones of the stone buildings. A group of young boys, no more than twelve or thirteen, huddled in the corner, their faces aglow with a mixture of excitement and anxiety as they clutched a tattered deck of cards, the edges worn from use. Among them was a boy named Leonardo, the youngest of the group. With dark hair that curled rebelliously around his ears and eyes that sparkled with a mixture of curiosity and mischief, he was the undisputed leader. He had a natural charm that drew others to him, a magnetic presence that made even the most jaded of adults smile. The other boys looked to him for guidance, for it was Leonardo who had suggested they come to the market that day, to seek out the fabled tarocchi deck that had recently arrived in the city.
