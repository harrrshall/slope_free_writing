The sun beat down on the parched earth of New Frontier, a world that was once a lush expanse of green but had since turned into a desolate landscape of dust and cracked soil. The year was 2145, and the old world was but a distant memory, lost to the annals of history. Here, the remnants of humanity had learned to adapt to a life where water was more precious than gold, and the air was thick with the scent of scorched earth. In the heart of this desolate land stood the town of Dustfall, a ramshackle settlement of metal and wood, where the structures seemed to lean into one another, as if seeking solace in their shared misfortune. The streets were a patchwork of cracked concrete and sandy dunes, and the only signs of life were the tattered flags flapping in the hot, dry wind. The town was a hub for traders, outlaws, and those seeking to escape their past, a place where the law was as flexible as the moral compass of its inhabitants. As the sun reached its peak, a lone figure appeared on the horizon, a dark silhouette against the harsh brightness. He rode a horse, a majestic creature that seemed out of place in this barren world, its coat a deep chestnut that glistened with a sheen of sweat. The rider, a man named Cade, was a figure known only to those who had crossed his path. His hat, wide and weathered, cast a shadow over his face, and the scar running down his cheek was a testament to the hard life he had lived. Cade was a gunslinger, a drifter, and a man with a past he preferred to keep buried. He carried a holster at his side, a weapon that had seen as much blood as the man himself, and the weight of it was a comfort. As he neared Dustfall, he could see the townsfolk going about their day. They were a mix of scavengers and traders, their faces hard and their eyes sharper. Cade's presence did not go unnoticed; the townsfolk paused, their eyes narrowing as they took in the stranger. He ignored their gazes, the weight of their judgment a familiar burden. He was here for one reason, and one reason only: to find the man they called the Water King.
