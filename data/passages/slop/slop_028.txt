In the heart of the Industrial Revolution, smoke stacked the skies over the bustling town of Ironford. The clamor of factories echoed day and night, a symphony of progress that never ceased. Amidst the coal-stained streets and the rhythmic pulse of machinery, a young woman named Emily tended her father's small flower shop, a vibrant oasis amidst the gray. Her hands, though calloused from work, were gentle as she arranged roses, their petals a soft caress against her skin. Emily's life was one of routine, dictated by the sun's rise and the factory whistles that marked the day's ebb and flow. She lived above the shop with her father, a man with a heart as big as his bushy white beard. He was a man of few words, but his eyes always crinkled with pride when he looked at his daughter. One day, a new face appeared at the flower shop. A young man with eyes as dark as the coal that fueled the town, and hands that bore the stains of his work as a factory engineer. His name was William, and he came bearing a single, perfect red rose, its stem wrapped in crisp, white paper. "For my mother," he explained, his voice carrying a hint of apology. He was new to town, he said, transferred from a city miles away. His mother, he confessed, was lonely in their new home, and he hoped the rose would brighten her day. Emily's heart, unaccustomed to such gestures, skipped a beat. She took the rose, its velvety petals a stark contrast against the grime that clung to her hands. "It will make her happy," she assured him, her voice softer than she intended. William's gaze lingered, his eyes raking over the shop, taking in the colors and scents that filled the small space. "You have a gift," he said, looking back at her. "This place... it's beautiful." Emily blushed, a warm hue spreading across her cheeks. "Thank you," she whispered, handing him the rose. Their fingers brushed briefly, a spark igniting between them, unseen, unheard, but felt. Days turned into weeks, and William returned to the flower shop with regularity.
