In the heart of Renaissance Italy, in the city of Venice, where canals whispered secrets and marble palaces stood as testaments to human ambition, a crime had been committed that threatened to unravel the delicate threads of society. The year was 1502, and the grandest of all the city-states, the Republic of Venice, was in turmoil. The morning sun had barely begun to kiss the waterline when the body of Lorenzo Veneziano, the most renowned and respected merchant of the city, was found floating in the Grand Canal. His face, once handsome and full of life, was now a mask of terror, frozen in an eternal gasp. The news spread like a plague, leaving a trail of fear and confusion in its wake. The watchmen, their red caps pulled low over their eyes, brought the body to the nearest bridge. The crowd that gathered was a mix of curiosity and horror. Among them was Marco, a young artisan with a heart for justice, and his sister, Isabella, whose eyes burned with a fire only sibling love could ignite.
